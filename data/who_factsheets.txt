WHO Fact Sheet: Pain Management
Paracetamol is a common analgesic and antipyretic drug used for the relief of fever, headaches, and other minor aches and pains. Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs are used for pain, fever and inflammation but carry a higher risk of stomach and kidney side effects with regular use than paracetamol.

WHO Fact Sheet: Cardiovascular Health (Hypertension)
Hypertension is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease and often causes no symptoms, so regular blood pressure checks matter. ACE inhibitors (such as lisinopril), ARBs (such as losartan), diuretics, calcium channel blockers and beta-blockers are common classes of blood pressure medication. NSAIDs such as ibuprofen can raise blood pressure and reduce the effect of these medicines, and should be used cautiously alongside them.

WHO Fact Sheet: Antimicrobial Resistance
Antibiotics such as amoxicillin work only on bacterial infections, not on colds, flu or most sore throats, which are usually viral. Overuse and misuse of antibiotics drives antimicrobial resistance, making future infections harder to treat. Always complete a prescribed antibiotic course as directed.

WHO Fact Sheet: Diabetes
Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition in which blood sugar rises because the body cannot use insulin effectively. Metformin is a first-line medicine for type 2 diabetes and is usually combined with diet and physical activity. Kidney function should be monitored while taking metformin, and it may need to be paused around certain scans or during illness with dehydration.

WHO Fact Sheet: Generic Medicines
A generic medicine contains the same active ingredient, in the same strength and form, as the branded original, and must meet the same quality and effectiveness standards. Generic medicines are typically cheaper because manufacturers do not repeat the original clinical trials. Always confirm the active ingredient and strength with a pharmacist before switching brands.
