Drug Information Leaflet: Over-the-Counter and Common Prescription Medicines

1. Paracetamol (Tylenol)
- Uses: Temporary relief of mild-to-moderate pain and fever reduction.
- Warnings: Do not exceed 4000 mg in 24 hours for adults. Overdose can cause severe, sometimes silent liver damage that may not cause symptoms until it is serious. Avoid regular heavy alcohol use while taking it.
- Common side effects: Rare at label doses; occasional nausea or rash.
- Interactions: Regular use alongside warfarin can increase its blood-thinning effect; carbamazepine can reduce paracetamol's effectiveness.

2. Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) and other NSAIDs (naproxen/Aleve, aspirin)
- Uses: Anti-inflammatory, analgesic and fever-reducing effects; used for arthritis, muscle strain, dental pain and period pain.
- Warnings: Take with food or milk to reduce stomach irritation. Use caution with a history of stomach ulcers, kidney disease, heart failure or high blood pressure. Do not combine two different NSAIDs at the same time.
- Interactions: Can blunt the blood-pressure-lowering effect of ACE inhibitors (such as lisinopril), ARBs (such as losartan), diuretics and beta-blockers, and can increase bleeding risk when combined with anticoagulants such as warfarin or with aspirin.

3. Lisinopril (Prinivil, Zestril) and Losartan (Cozaar) - blood pressure medication
- Uses: Treatment of high blood pressure and heart failure management. ACE inhibitors (lisinopril) and ARBs (losartan) work on the same hormone system and are generally not combined with each other except under specialist care.
- Warnings: Do not take during pregnancy. May raise blood potassium; monitor levels, and avoid potassium supplements unless advised. Do not stop suddenly without medical guidance.
- Common side effects: Persistent dry cough (more common with ACE inhibitors), dizziness, lightheadedness.

4. Atorvastatin (Lipitor)
- Uses: Lowers LDL cholesterol and triglycerides; reduces the risk of stroke and heart attack.
- Warnings: Avoid excessive grapefruit juice, which can raise blood levels of the drug. Report unexplained muscle aches or weakness promptly, as this can rarely signal a serious muscle problem.
- Interactions: Certain antibiotics (such as clarithromycin) and antifungals (such as itraconazole) can increase the risk of muscle-related side effects.

5. Warfarin (Coumadin) - a blood thinner (anticoagulant)
- Uses: Prevents and treats harmful blood clots.
- Warnings: Needs regular INR blood testing. Many medicines (including NSAIDs, aspirin and some antibiotics), herbal products and sudden changes in vitamin-K-rich foods affect it; check with your anticoagulant clinic before starting or stopping anything, including paracetamol used regularly.

6. Amoxicillin (Amoxil) - an antibiotic
- Uses: Treats certain bacterial infections such as ear, throat, chest, urinary and skin infections.
- Warnings: Do not use if allergic to penicillin. Has no effect on colds, flu or other viral illnesses. Complete the full course as prescribed even if symptoms improve early.

7. Cetirizine (Zyrtec) and Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) - antihistamines
- Uses: Relieve allergy symptoms such as hay fever, runny nose, itchy eyes and hives; diphenhydramine is also used short-term for sleeplessness.
- Warnings: May cause drowsiness (more pronounced with diphenhydramine); take care when driving and limit alcohol.

8. Omeprazole (Prilosec) - a proton pump inhibitor
- Uses: Reduces stomach acid for heartburn, acid reflux and stomach ulcers.
- Warnings: Long-term use can lower vitamin B12 and magnesium levels; use only for as long as directed, and see a doctor if heartburn persists beyond the labelled period.

9. Metformin (Glucophage) - for type 2 diabetes
- Uses: Controls blood sugar in type 2 diabetes, usually alongside diet and activity changes.
- Warnings: Kidney problems, dehydration or heavy alcohol use may require pausing it; tell your doctor before a contrast-dye scan.
